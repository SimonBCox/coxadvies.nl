From a report on a house in Amsterdam

A ground floor apartment in Amsterdam Oud Zuid. On the left what the Dutch registers give, in their own language. On the right what a structural engineer reads in it.

What the registers give What an engineer reads in it
BAGBouwjaar: 1886
Amsterdam buildings of this age stand, as a rule, on timber piles. Those last as long as they stay below the groundwater. When it drops, the tops rot, and the repair means a new foundation.
KlimaateffectatlasRisico paalrot, Sarphatiparkbuurt: 15%
This is a figure for the neighbourhood, not for this building. Whether this foundation was investigated or repaired is not public. Ask the owners' association.
StadsarchiefSix building files, 1912 to 2004. 2003: berekeningen en rapportages.
The 2003 file includes a structural calculation, so structural work was done then. Ask the seller what it was, or read the files.
Bouwdossiers2004 splitsing
Split into apartments in 2004. The foundation, the roof and the facades are then normally shared by all owners, so a foundation repair is a shared bill. Ask the owners' association for its maintenance plan and reserve fund.
Gemeente AmsterdamBeschermd stadsgezicht Oud Zuid, 30-11-2022
Not a monument, but the city can set extra rules for the outside. Check them before you plan new windows or a dormer.

All five lines are public. Together they say that this house most likely stands on timber piles, that structural work was done on it in 2003, and that you share the foundation with the other owners. The address report gives you that picture for any address, with the questions that follow from it.

And what the files said once they were read

The report lists the files. For € 50 more they are requested from the archive, read and explained. For this house, that turned up three things the index does not show.

  • A level survey from 2002. An engineering firm measured the front facade and the first floor. The facade drops 12 mm across the width of the house, the floor 34 mm at one point. In all six files it is the only document about the foundation. A new measurement at the same points shows whether anything has moved since.
  • Balconies built before the permit. The four balconies at the back went up in 2003 without a permit. The building inspector stopped the work, and the permit was granted on 19 November 2003. The calculation covers the balcony beams, not the wall they hang from.
  • A folder that is not what its name says. In the index a 2002 folder is called Berekeningen (calculations). It holds a copy of the 2003 balcony calculation. None of the files has a calculation for the floors or the foundation.

Questions

What buyers ask

Is this a building survey?

No. A building survey (bouwkundige keuring) is an inspection of the house itself. The report reads the records on the house and explains them, so you know what to look for during the survey and what to ask the seller.

Will it tell me whether the foundation is sound?

No, and no desk study can. It tells you what the house most likely stands on, how big the risk is in the neighbourhood, what the archive shows and which document would settle it. Certainty about one foundation takes a survey of that foundation.

My valuation report gives a foundation class. Why do I need this?

Since 1 April 2026 the valuer states a class from A to E, from a national model that is not public. The class says how big the risk is. The report tells you why, and in class D or E it tells you which documents can take the valuer's doubt away.

Does it work outside Amsterdam?

Yes, for any address in the Netherlands. Amsterdam publishes an index of building files per building, so there the report lists each file with its year and contents. Elsewhere it says which archive holds the files, per period, and how to get them.
